\begin{abstract}
As cybersecurity threats transition from static credential vulnerabilities to machine-speed autonomous Large Language Model (LLM) agent swarms, traditional single-entity risk frameworks (such as FAIR and ISO 27005) fail to model multi-sided, cross-platform financial destruction. In this paper, we establish \textbf{Synapticide's Law}, a fundamental theorem governing machine-speed cognitive network collapse, and introduce the \textbf{Systemic AI Cascade Loss (SACL)} mathematical model. We categorize cyber-adversarial dynamics across three evolutionary regimes: Human-vs-Human ($H \to H$), Human-vs-AI ($H \to A$), and AI-vs-AI ($A \to A$). By formalizing temporal decay parameters ($\Phi(t)$), discrete automated circuit breakers ($K_{\text{cb}}$), and swarm coordination density exponents ($\alpha_{\text{swarm}}$), we provide a closed-form integration for ecosystem loss. To validate our model, we executed a 10,000-run Monte Carlo simulation incorporating empirical baseline parameters. Our empirical results demonstrate heavy-tailed Power-Law dynamics: while the median breach loss yields \$258.24M USD, the 95th-percentile tail risk ($Value\text{-}at\text{-}Risk_{95\%}$) escalates to \$61.18B USD. This work establishes that machine-speed execution eliminates human-in-the-loop containment buffers, shifting loss dynamics from extortion payouts to algorithmic compute destruction, model retraining, and systemic B2B supply chain failure.
\end{abstract}
